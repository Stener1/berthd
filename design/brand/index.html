shipyard

A harbour for your agents.

A shipyard is where boats are built and tied up. In Shipyard, your boxes are harbours, each worktree is a berth (a place for one boat to tie up), and agents are the boats that come and go. The amber dot from the logo is the buoy: the one light that says something needs you.

01 · The idea

One metaphor, used lightly

The harbour shows up in names, in small drawings and in one colour, never as nautical copy. Labels in the app stay plain: worktree, agent, box. The metaphor sits underneath, so it only shows on quiet screens.

BoxA harbourA VPS or dev machine where the work is moored. When it goes out of reach, fog comes in.
WorktreeA berthOne place to tie up: its own branch, ports and terminals. Empty when nothing runs there.
AgentA boatMoored and ready, setting out on a task, or rafted up with others.
Amber dotA buoy, a lanternThe one point of colour. In the app it means waiting on you; in a drawing it is the light you look for.
LaptopThe shoreWhere you watch from. Closing it doesn't sink anything: the boats stay out.
FlowsA chartA course plotted ahead of time, sailed when its trigger fires.

03 · Colour

Quiet neutrals, one signal

  • Amber means "needs you": a waiting agent, an unread count, a box asking to be paired. The sidebar sample here is the only amber on this board outside the mark and the drawings.
  • In drawings, one amber light per scene: the buoy, the lamp or the sun. Its reflection on the water, or the lighthouse's beam, is the same light, not a second one.
  • Never amber for decoration, links, tags, highlights or progress. Busy is grey; done is green; "recommended" is a neutral outline.
  • One amber for the dot everywhere: #e8a33d in the icon, the favicon and Shipyard Dark's --warning (#c27a12 in Shipyard Light).
  • Every colour comes from the theme (--warning, currentColor), so Midnight, Paper and user themes get their own amber.

State is never a stripe down one side

A coloured left border or inset shadow on a card or row bends round its rounded corners and reads as decoration. Tint the whole outline, or say the state with a glyph or a word. The app keeps the same rule (app/src/components/ui/card.tsx); so do this board and the site.

05 · Illustration

The drawing language

Small line drawings on a strip of water, drawn once in code (app/src/components/art/scenes.tsx) and coloured by the theme. They sit where a screen would otherwise be empty, and nowhere else.

Canvas
160 × 52 units, waterline at y 49.4. Shown at 96–152px wide; 136px by default.
Strokes
2.8 deck · 2.4 pilings · 2.2 hulls · 1.4–1.8 detail · 1.4 water. Round caps and joins.
Palette
currentColor (muted text) for every line, 0.04–0.12 fills, and one amber light (with its reflection) from --warning.
Depth
Things below the water draw at ~26% opacity; water fades out at both ends, so a scene has no edges.
Motion
Transforms and opacity only. 4–10s loops, ≤2px and ≤10°, seamless. Rain is the one faster thing.
Restraint
One scene per screen, never in lists. Pauses offscreen, when the window is hidden, and behind dialogs; still under reduced motion.

08 · Directions

Where it could go

Four directions. A is what ships today; B to D are ways to take the harbour further. Each keeps the amber rule.

A · Harbour by day Recommended

What ships today. Plain line work on the app's own ground, one amber light, motion you notice on the second look. Fits a dense tool and every theme.

B · Harbour at night

Deep blue ground, stars, every amber dot glowing like a lantern. Beautiful on Midnight, heavy everywhere else, and the glow competes with real "needs you" signals.

C · Chart and tide lines

Depth contours, soundings in mono, dotted courses. Precise and technical; right for automations and flows, too cold for "nothing to review".

D · Signal flags

Flag colours as a status vocabulary. Bold and graphic, but it needs four or five saturated colours, which breaks the one-signal rule.

Recommendation: A, borrowing C for flows

Keep the harbour by day as the system, in the app and on the site: the landing page's hero is the same daylight harbour (a pale sun in line, no moon), with the masthead lamp of the boat that needs you as its one amber light. Use the chart only where the app is about plans (flows, runs), and save the night palette for the Midnight theme's splash, if anywhere.

Shipyard brand board. The scenes, the gallery, the map and the scene styles are generated from app/src/components/art/ and the app's <Scene> placements: run node design/brand/build-scenes.mjs after changing either (--check says whether the board is current).

Serve the repository's root to view it (fonts come from site/assets/fonts). Motion stops if your system asks for reduced motion.
